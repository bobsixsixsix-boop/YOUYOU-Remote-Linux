Reading: same build, same settings, same host, two minutes apart, 99.52 % vs 95.92 %. The
phase column says why. In n1 frames left the decoder about 3 ms after the previous flip; in
n2 about 5.8 ms, nearly all 30 seconds (host and panel clocks barely drifted). 5.8 ms sits
at the point where KWin decides what goes into the next refresh, so small network jitter
flips frames between "in time" and "late": a repeat, then two frames in one refresh, one of
them skipped. Where that phase lands is luck of the session, not the player (decoder to
swap done is 0.4-0.5 ms in both). Seconds 3-5 of n2 are a network burst on top (14 and 8
repeats at phase 2.6-2.7). Both cases are what Moonlight's frame pacer handles: hold one
frame and present it at a fixed point in the refresh. Next: pacer A/B, several runs each.
